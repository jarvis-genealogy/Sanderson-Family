Francis SANDERSON

Death date: 22nd February 1864

Details

The voyage

The "Omega". Built in 1850, 765 tons.
The voyage of the Omega from London to Adelaide took 87 days or just under three months. Although arrival was recorded at Port Adelaide, the shallow water was a problem for larger vessels and Holdfast Bay, now Glenelg, was a safe anchorage and a convenient place for the emigrants to disembark, so Francis, Hannah and their six children came ashore there.

The Glenelg jetty was not built until 1859 so there was no convenient wharf for a large sailing ship to draw up. The facilities for receiving a shipload of immigrants were primitive and passengers had to be transferred to smaller boats. The surf prevented them from coming directly onto dry land so they were helped or carried through the water by the sailors, often on their backs. The luggage was an even greater problem. Trunks, boxes and personal possessions had to be unloaded and carried through the water, then deposited above the high-water mark.

On reaching the beach, the new arrivals found themselves in a landscape quite unlike the towns and villages they had left behind in England. Beyond the white sandy beach were low sandhills, with scattered scrub and trees. Beyond these lay open country. A flagstaff and guns marked the government presence, while small huts and stores served the needs of those arriving and those already living in the settlement.

Bullock drays and carts were the principal means of transporting luggage and goods inland. Teams were not always readily available, and families sometimes had to wait while their possessions were gathered together. Getting themselves and their belongings safely ashore was only the beginning. They now had to find accommodation.

Francis was relatively wealthy, as the proprietor of Iron and Steel works at Newcastle on Tyne, and before coming to Australia, he sold the works and his home. They travelled as fare paying passengers, indicating sufficient private means for the family to establish themselves in the colony. So it's unlikely that they went to basic immigrant accommodation on arrival. Private accommodation could have been arranged short term. The Glenelg Hotel is one possibility, or they could have rented a furnished house or cottage in Glenelg or travelled into Adelaide and stayed in a better-appointed lodging. The family eventually lived in Medindie and the youngest, Francis Clement Villiers, continued his education at St Peter's College in Adelaide.
